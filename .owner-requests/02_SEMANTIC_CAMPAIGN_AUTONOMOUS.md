# 6. Per-root semantic research

For every root:

## 6.1 Internal discovery

## 6.2 Root semantic unity

A root concept is not acceptable unless its contribution is demonstrably present in **every confirmed occurrence**.

No majority bypass exists.

If one occurrence fails:

* challenge the concept;
* challenge structural attribution;
* challenge layer separation;
* challenge the analysis;
* generate a better candidate.

If unresolved:

`UNRESOLVED`

Do not invent exceptions.

---

## 6.3 Layer derivation

For representative and hard occurrences preserve:

```text
root meaning contribution
→ derivation / inflection contribution
→ form contribution
→ construction / participant contribution
→ context / discourse contribution
→ local meaning
→ final semantic statement
```

The internal methodology already requires separating root contribution from morphology, derivation, construction, participants, discourse, and local context.

---

## 6.4 Competing hypotheses

Do not test only the preferred hypothesis.

Generate serious competitors.

Record the strongest competitor.

---

## 6.5 Falsification

Attempt to defeat the preferred root concept.

Record:

* strongest counterexample;
* rejection conditions;
* weakening conditions;
* unresolved cases;
* failed predictions;
* reopen conditions.

---

## 6.6 Holdout / prediction

Where feasible:

withhold occurrences or clusters from hypothesis construction.

Freeze the hypothesis.

Record predictions.

Reveal the held-out data.

Evaluate without silently rewriting the original hypothesis.

---

## 6.7 Semantic-neighbor challenge

Test whether the proposed root meaning is sufficiently distinctive from the nearest relevant roots/expressions.

Universal applicability without distinctiveness is insufficient.
